## Your phone

- **A new look, made for the phone.** Every screen is redesigned: the computer on its own card, a clear step-by-step
  pairing with the code in large boxes, a status dot, the agent and the workspace on every row, and a notice that
  says what to do whenever something needs your attention. The workspace chip uses the colour you gave it on the
  computer.
- **An Agents tab.** Every agent working on your computer in one list, conversations and terminal agents together,
  filtered by **Needs you**, **Working**, **Failed** and **Finished**. Opening a finished one marks it as seen on the
  computer too.
- **Read a terminal agent's conversation.** When Claude Code or Codex runs in a terminal, switch between **Screen**
  and **Conversation** to read what you and the agent wrote, without the terminal noise. It's read only; to type, go
  back to **Screen**.
- **Steer an agent while it works.** While a conversation is running, the field reads "Steer: read after the current
  step" and **Send** delivers your instruction when the step ends. **Stop the turn** stays one tap away, and asks
  first.
- **Requests where you need them.** A request waiting in a conversation shows at the top, and inside the
  conversation as a card with **Deny** and **Review**. The risk badge reads at a glance: green for low, blue for
  medium, amber for high, solid red for critical.
- **Settings on the phone.** Choose the theme (same as the phone, light or dark) and which notifications you get:
  an agent needs you, a task finished, a task failed. They're kept on the phone only.

## Safe answers

- **Every answer asks for your fingerprint or screen lock**, Deny included, so nobody else can answer from your
  phone.
- **At critical risk, Allow once waits 2 seconds** with a countdown ring, the same pause the computer gives you.
- **A request too long to show in full can only be denied on the phone.** A question that was cut short is answered
  on the computer. The phone never lets you approve what it couldn't show you.

## Terminals and privacy

- **Choose which terminals your phone sees.** On the computer, a terminal's **Show on phone** switch hides it from
  the phone, and **Allow typing from the phone** decides whether the phone can type in it. A terminal hidden while
  you're watching it closes on the phone, shows only "Terminal" in its title, and keeps nothing of what was on screen.
- **Wide terminals fit.** A terminal wider than the phone shows "80 cols · swipe to pan"; drag sideways to read it,
  or use **Fit the width** in the text size menu.

## Pairing

- **A pairing that expired before the phone connected now says so** ("This pairing expired before the phone
  connected. Pair again."), instead of reading as removed by the computer.

## Phones still on 1.4.3

OXESpace 1.5.0 on the computer still speaks to OxeMobile 1.4.3. A phone you haven't updated keeps working with
everything it already did; the new screens above arrive with the 1.5.0 app. If a future computer version ever stops
accepting an older app, the phone says so and asks you to update the app or OXESpace on your computer, instead of
showing an empty screen.

## Verify the Android APK

Before installing `oxespace-android-v1.5.0.apk`, check that it's the published file and that the project's key
signed it:

1. `sha256sum -c oxespace-android-v1.5.0.apk.sha256` (both files are in this release's assets).
   On Windows: `Get-FileHash oxespace-android-v1.5.0.apk` in PowerShell, and compare it with the value in the
   `.sha256` file.
2. `apksigner verify --print-certs oxespace-android-v1.5.0.apk` (Android SDK build-tools). The line
   `Signer #1 certificate SHA-256 digest:` must read `cc65d90afa2fde6518adad92d40769496a043b901f603eff0562e056cd4336e4`.

The signing certificate's SHA-256 fingerprint is:

`CC:65:D9:0A:FA:2F:DE:65:18:AD:AD:92:D4:07:69:49:6A:04:3B:90:1F:60:3E:FF:05:62:E0:56:CD:43:36:E4`

It's the same key as 1.4.3, so the app updates in place. The full steps are in
[Verifying an APK before installing it](https://github.com/propagno/oxespace/blob/main/mobile/android/RELEASING.md#verifying-an-apk-before-installing-it).
